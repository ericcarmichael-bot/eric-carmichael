# Jones Act / Hawaiʻi Freight — AI-Assisted Source Pack

**Purpose:** Research leads and verified factual source notes for Eric's individual research paper. This file is not the paper, the analysis, or the recommendation. Every item used in the paper should be re-checked against the linked source.

## Research question in the existing decision memo

Does the evidence support the hypothesis that allowing foreign-flag carriers to transport domestic cargo between Honolulu and U.S. West Coast ports would reduce container freight rates?

## Sources worth using

### 1. Coastwise legal restriction

**46 U.S.C. § 55102 — Transportation of merchandise**

Primary legal source for the coastwise restriction on transporting merchandise between U.S. points.

Source: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title46-section55102&num=0&edition=prelim

Use for: defining the entry restriction. Do not use the statute itself as evidence of the size of any price effect.

### 2. Matson's description of Hawaiʻi competition

**Matson 2025 Form 10-K**

Matson identifies Pasha Hawaii as its principal U.S.-flag competitor in the Hawaiʻi trade and discusses Jones Act changes as a competitive risk that could permit lower-cost competitors to enter.

Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/3453/000110465926020944/matx-20251231x10k.htm

Use for: current market structure and the incumbent's own description of the entry mechanism.

### 3. Matson published directional rates

**Matson household-goods tariffs**

2025 40-foot port-to-port base ocean freight:
- West Coast → Honolulu: $7,276
- Honolulu → West Coast: $4,254
- Westbound premium: about 71.0%

2026 40-foot port-to-port base ocean freight:
- West Coast → Honolulu: $7,476
- Honolulu → West Coast: $4,354
- Westbound premium: about 71.7%

Sources:
- 2025: https://www.matson.com/others/Hawaii_Service_June_1_2025.pdf
- 2026: https://www.matson.com/others/HHG_Hawaii_6.26.pdf

Use for: descriptive evidence that direction/backhaul economics matter.

Important limitation: these are published household-goods tariff rates, not market-wide average commercial rates and not a causal estimate of a "Jones Act premium."

### 4. Direct Hawaiʻi evidence that competition changed realized pricing

**American President Lines, LLC v. Matson, Inc. — D.D.C. opinion (2025)**

The factual record describes Matson's 2016 Hawaiʻi household-goods loyalty program, created to compete with Pasha:
- 15% discount for customers placing 90% of Hawaiʻi HHG volume with Matson.
- The program later expanded to 20% for meeting the threshold in Hawaiʻi or Guam and 25% for meeting it in both.

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/district-of-columbia/dcdce/1:2021cv02040/233910/195/

Use for: evidence that competitive pressure can change realized prices in the Hawaiʻi trade.

Legal caution: the court granted summary judgment to Matson. The source can support factual observations about pricing and competition; it should not be characterized as a finding that Matson acted unlawfully.

### 5. Guam as a near-neighbor mechanism comparison

The same federal court record describes APL's entry into the U.S.–Guam trade:
- Matson's U.S.–Guam share declined from 94% in 2016 to 82% in 2017 and 74% in 2018.
- Home Depot documents described APL's service as roughly 40% cheaper but about seven days slower, and Home Depot initially awarded APL only 10% of its Guam volume.
- Matson's former VP of Pricing testified that Matson sometimes adjusted Pacific Northwest prices in response to APL's Oakland pricing.

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/district-of-columbia/dcdce/1:2021cv02040/233910/195/

Use for: mechanism evidence that entry can affect market share and incumbent pricing.

Important limitation: Guam is not a clean treatment group for Hawaiʻi. Route structure, transit time, legal treatment, service quality, and other conditions differ.

### 6. Puerto Rico comparator

**GAO-13-260 — Puerto Rico: Characteristics of the Island's Maritime Trade and Potential Effects of Modifying the Jones Act**

GAO reported that shippers it interviewed often, though not always, cited lower foreign-carrier rates for similar cargo moving to or from foreign ports, even over longer distances. GAO could not validate the examples or quantify a market-wide differential.

Source: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-13-260

Use for: independent government comparator evidence and an example of how carefully to state limitations.

## Best evidence hierarchy for the paper

1. Direct evidence that competitive pressure changed pricing behavior in Hawaiʻi.
2. Near-neighbor evidence that entry changed market share and incumbent pricing.
3. Evidence that foreign carriers could plausibly participate in the network and that the restriction limits the feasible carrier set.
4. Current tariffs as descriptive context and a check on directional/backhaul economics.

Tariff archaeology should not become the paper. The causal question is competition and entry, not which published line item is largest.
