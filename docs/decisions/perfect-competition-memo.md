# Perfect Competition — recommendation

**Recommendation.** Plant 10 beds of tomatoes, 20 of carrots and 30 of mesclun, and leave 4 beds empty. This mix earns $42,761.66 for the season. This is the mix that maximizes the profit for the farm. Keep planting a bed only while it brings in more than it costs to grow, and the 11th tomato bed doesn't.

## Why

- **Tomatoes stop at 10.** Every extra tomato bed takes more hours than the last. Bed 10 costs $8,249 to grow and sells for $8,800. Bed 11 costs $9,391, so it loses $591.
- **Carrots and mesclun stop at their limits, not their costs.** Their last beds still make money (+$405 and +$280). They stop only because 20 and 30 beds are the most we're allowed to plant.
- **Don't cut a crop just because it "loses money" alone.** Charged the full $20,000 fixed cost on its own, each looks like a loser. That $20,000 gets paid whether they're planted or not, and together they put $29,618 toward it.

## The judgment call

If we can get more ground for a capped crop, carrots come first. The next carrot bed adds $352 of profit, against $246 for mesclun. I'd pay up to $352 for the first extra carrot bed this season, and a little less for each one after (bed 22 is worth about $298). The upside runs out fast: with only 4 empty beds, it stops at 24 carrot beds, for +$1,076 in total. Don't spend on more general land or a fifth worker. We're already leaving 4 beds and most of one worker's hours unused.

## What would change my answer

Tomato price. If it falls below about $8,250 a bed (6% lower), I'd cut tomatoes to 9 beds; a 20% drop takes it to 8.
